Thread 01 / 04 · AI deception

Unmasking the Shadows of AI — Deceptive Capabilities in LLMs

Inspired by critical questions raised during the 2023 AI Safety Summit, I began exploring AI deception — how different forms of deceptive capabilities entangle with historical and societal biases, and how discourses around deception evolve alongside technological development.

Drawing on Science and Technology Studies to understand the power dynamics shaping technological paradigms, I evaluated AI deception's social implications and persistent challenges — published on arXiv during my undergraduate studies.

Thread 02 / 04 · Algorithmic audit

Unmasking Artificial Intimacy — A Critical Audit of Microsoft's XiaoIce

At the Oxford Internet Institute I studied Fairness, Transparency and Accountability in Machine Learning.

I applied these tools in a qualitative audit of Microsoft's XiaoIce, an AI companion released in 2014, years before Character.ai and Replika.

Following ACM FAccT's auditing principles, the audit drew on interdisciplinary theory to examine the model's gendered design and the power dynamics built into its affordances.

Thread 03 / 04 · Oxford MSc thesis

The Velvet Cage of AI Companions — Illusions of Intimacy and Control

My Oxford thesis asked how, and why, people come to see AI companions as trustworthy 'social actors'.

Using mixed methods — quantitative dataset analysis and qualitative interviews — I studied both romantic and non-romantic users' experiences.

I traced the mechanisms of connectedness, moments of anthropomorphisation, and the cyclical fulfilment of dual needs that can foster compulsive use, and how non-judgemental self-disclosure to an AI may reshape what people expect from relationships and love.

The abstract is available on request.

Thread 04 / 04 · Policy

Policy Recommendations to the Cyberspace Administration of China

Building on my thesis, I submitted policy recommendations to the Cyberspace Administration of China, informing its provisional framework for increasingly human-like AI services.

Four priorities: measurable indicators of users' emotional dependency; tiered intervention for users in high-risk psychological states; clear in-interface disclosure; and oversight of anthropomorphic design so it cannot exploit psychological vulnerabilities for engagement.

The full recommendations are available on request.

About

Hi, I'm Grace.

I'm a sociologist studying the social impact of digital technologies through a sociotechnical lens. With a particular interest in AI, I look at how its infrastructure works underneath, to inform ethics from a sociological perspective.

I studied Sociology at UCL, then completed an MSc in Sociology at the University of Oxford, with technical grounding at the Oxford Internet Institute in Fairness, Transparency and Accountability of Machine Learning.

I work where AI companions, AI deception and platform governance meet, and I turn what I learn into tools people can use.
